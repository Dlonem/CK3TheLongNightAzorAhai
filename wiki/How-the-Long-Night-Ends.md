# How the Long Night Ends

*Part of the [AGOT: The Long Night & Azor Ahai](Home) wiki. On dlonem.com: [How it ends](https://dlonem.com/mods/the-long-night/#ending).*

Since version 1.1.0 the Night King is fought, not rolled. This page is the whole road from the first won battle to the last blow, and what happens if you lose.

## 1. Beat the dead in the field once

The **War for the Dawn** cannot be declared until the dead have been beaten: a war they lose opens it, and so does casting the Night King down in the field with ordinary steel. That is true on every difficulty. Only an **independent ruler** may declare it; vassals join their liege's.

The dead do not stop while you prepare. On Weak and Standard a lost war freezes them for one to three years; on Strong and Insane, six to eighteen months. That window is what you get.

## 2. Reach him

The Night King leads his host in person. Win the battle he is standing in — as its commander, or as a knight fighting under someone who cannot — and the next day *The Field Afterwards* opens for you. His host is broken and scattered across the ice, and he has not moved. The card reads differently depending on what you brought: Lightbringer, Valyrian steel, dragonglass, a dragon overhead, a dragon the dead took from you, or nothing at all.

The choices:

- **End it.** Go to him. Opens the Last Hour. Shown greyed if you carry nothing that can hurt him.
- **We do not go to him alone — form the line.** Bring your companions. Also opens the Last Hour, with your line drafted and his built.
- **I have already seen how this goes.** The greenseer's option, for a champion with greensight or dragon dreams who also carries a bane — foresight buys better odds, not a win.
- **Go out there anyway. Somebody has to be first.** What a man carrying nothing that can hurt him is offered instead of the swing. It costs him.
- **Sound the retreat. Not today, and not with this.** Live to try again.

## 3. The Last Hour

A Trial-of-Seven melee in the snow, on the ground the battle was fought on. Your line is you and the people who were actually there: companions drafted from your knights and commanders, kin, friends, sworn brothers if you are one. His line is him and his White Walkers — one beside him on Weak, two on Standard, three on Strong and Insane — and, in a later fight, any champion he has raised before.

What decides it:

- **Prowess counts for half.** Every living fighter loses roughly half his prowess the moment the melee opens and gets it back, with interest, only for what he is carrying. A great swordsman with castle steel loses eventually. That is the point.
- **Castle steel does not bite.** It shatters. The record remembers that it did.
- **Valyrian steel holds.** It opens its own option and materially changes the exchange.
- **Dragonglass works, and is half as good as Valyrian steel.** Carrying only obsidian makes the fight harder, not impossible.
- **Lightbringer** — forged or consecrated — is the strongest blade in the fight and opens its own option.
- **Favored and Chosen of R'hllor** each open their own option too.
- **Your line can take the blow.** A brother, a sister, a parent, a lover, a spouse or a friend standing beside you may step in front of the stroke meant for you, or make their own attempt on him. Companions can be killed, can run, and can be carrying Valyrian steel of their own.
- **A red priest can pull you back.** If you have been given the Lord's Blessing and fall, your priest gets the argument. See [Azor Ahai and the Prophecy](Azor-Ahai-and-the-Prophecy#the-last-kiss).

## 4. Bring a bane

Only four things destroy him: **dragonglass, Valyrian steel, Lightbringer, or dragonfire.** Anything else — a won melee with ordinary steel, a won war on score with nothing in hand — casts him down. The dark crowns another, the new Night King inherits every bonus the old one had, and the Long Night goes on. Beating him in the field does still open the War for the Dawn.

When a bane lands, the Long Night ends. The war resolves, the dead die where they stand — wights, Others, undead dragons — and the reveal opens: whether the prophecy was ever true, who was actually the prince, and who was standing there when it happened. **Whoever landed the blow is Azor Ahai Reborn**, marks or no marks, named or not. An unnamed nobody can end the Long Night and prove every red priest in the world wrong. That is deliberate.

## 5. Lose, and he keeps you

Losing to him is worse than dying. Your character is raised as one of the Others *before* the card opens — the portrait is already wrong when you read it — and the card tells you what it felt like: almost no thoughts. That character is his from then on. You play on as your heir. A later champion who reaches the Last Hour may find your old character standing on the Night King's side of the line, because the best heroes who tried are the ones he keeps.

The other outcomes of a lost exchange: you die; you are pulled back by a red priest who has blessed you; or you get away, wounded, if the roll favours you. A White Walker never accepts a yield from the living.

## Before the Long Night: a Walker on the snowfield

During *Winter Is Coming*, before the dead have taken a single county, rangers and northern lords far enough north can meet a lone White Walker: a small melee, one Walker against a handful of men, with the Sam Tarly outcome in the deck. Killing a Walker is remembered on your character page, and a man who has killed three carries the dread of it. Losing one of these is not the end — you can flee, be taken, or be spared — but it tells you exactly what you are going to be facing.

## The record

The character page remembers all of it: the Walker you killed, the night you faced him and lived, the steel that broke in your hand, the day the Wall fell and where you were, who paid to bring you back and who they paid it for, the day the dark won, and the day it lifted. When the Long Night ends, the *men who were there* card names only the people who were actually in the fight. If you went alone, the record says so.

## Practical advice

- Get a red priest into your court before you go north, and ask him for the blessing. It is the difference between a lost exchange and a lost campaign.
- Equip the sword. A Valyrian blade in a vault does nothing; one in your hand opens its own option.
- Mine dragonglass early. It is a bane, it is cheap next to Valyrian steel, and a landless ruler can mine it too.
- Do not take a dragon over a standing Wall to meet him unless you are prepared to lose it. Beyond an intact Wall he is near-certain to take it.
- Form the line. Companions can die, and the roll call afterwards is honest about who stood — but a man alone against him and three Walkers is a man alone.
