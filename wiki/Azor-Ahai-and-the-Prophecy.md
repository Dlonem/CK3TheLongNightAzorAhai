# Azor Ahai and the Prophecy

*Part of the [AGOT: The Long Night & Azor Ahai](Home) wiki. On dlonem.com: [The prophecy](https://dlonem.com/mods/the-long-night/#prophecy).*

Nobody is born Azor Ahai. You accumulate marks, a holy man says your name out loud, and then you have to actually go and do it. Every character in the world is measured the same way, continuously, and the AI competes for the naming as hard as you do.

## The three rungs

| Rung | How you get it |
|---|---|
| **Favored of R'hllor** | Two marks, and someone qualified to name you |
| **Chosen of R'hllor** | Four marks and a namer — replaces Favored |
| **Azor Ahai Reborn** | Destroy the Night King. Nothing else grants it, ever |

The last row is the important one. No amount of prophecy, piety or priestly enthusiasm makes you Azor Ahai Reborn; you get it for landing the killing blow, whether or not a single priest ever said your name. An unnamed nobody can end the Long Night and prove every red priest in the world wrong. That is deliberate.

The *Naming* game rule moves the thresholds: *The Flames Are Loud* is one and three, *The Citadel's Reading* is three and six, *Nobody Is Ever Named* switches the ladder off. Claimants carry *the Prince That Was Promised* as a nickname.

## The nine marks

The *Read the Signs* decision costs nothing and tells you which you carry.

**Five are decided before you ever take control.** These are about who you pick.

1. **Dragon dreams.** AGOT marks children with this at birth. This mod reads it.
2. **Greensight.** Likewise — and a greenseer can name himself; nobody has to tell him what he is.
3. **Born amid salt and smoke.** A coastal birth in a world that still has living dragons in it. Common in Aegon's century, rare after the dragons die.
4. **Born beneath a bleeding star.** Born during Winter Is Coming or the Long Night itself. A child born into the dark is a different kind of claimant.
5. **Hidden blood.** A bastard of Valyrian descent whose father held a duchy or better, and who is not a known dragonseed. What separates Jon from Ulf is not the blood; it is that the blood was hidden inside a great house.

**Four you earn in play.**

6. **Valyrian steel.** It must be equipped, not sitting in a vault. Lightbringer counts. Lose the sword and you lose the mark, which can un-name you.
7. **Woke dragons out of stone.** Own a dragon. Lose it and the mark goes with it.
8. **Walked out of a fight with the dead.** Survive an encounter with the Others.
9. **Died and came back.** Carry *Resurrected by R'hllor*. Coming back is itself a mark, so it can push you up a rung at the exact moment a red priest is standing over you.

## You need a namer

Marks alone do nothing. Someone has to say it: a **red priest** (appoint an R'hllor courtier as court chaplain and AGOT gives them the trait; a Red Priestess on the council counts), a **witch**, a courtier or guest **with greensight**, or **your own greensight**. A landless red priest can also formally **sponsor** you — an interaction where they ask and you say yes. Presence and consent, in that order.

You do not have to be a king. A hedge knight or a courtier can be named over the head of the man who feeds him. Many people can be Favored at once, and rival claimants meet, and sometimes ally; the *Claimants* rule can restrict the naming to players, or to one Chosen at a time.

## Lightbringer

Two roads, one sword. **Forge it** — three temperings, the last of them the price every version of the tale agrees on: someone the champion actually loves. **Or consecrate** a Valyrian steel blade you already carry (added in 1.0.12), at the same price; the blade keeps its own name, history and house. Dawn is excluded from consecration and asks a different price of whoever carries it. One Lightbringer per world: completing either road closes the other. Whoever carries it counts as bearing a bane at the Last Hour, and it opens its own option in the melee.

## The last kiss

A red priest can spend himself on one person, once, ever. Ask for the Lord's Blessing (a decision, available whenever a priest who can give it is within reach — your court chaplain, a ranging company's camp priest, anyone in your hall), and if you fall in battle the fire gets the argument: a card when you go down, your priest's decision two days later, and coming back is your button to press. A refusal arrives the same way.

Being returned is not once per lifetime. Each return needs a fresh priest, takes more out of you, and climbs the *Resurrected by R'hllor* track — the Beric road. The world hears of about five such returns a year and ten once the dead are walking; asking for one yourself is never refused and never counts against that. Being Favored is itself a reason for the fire to want you back.

## The part nobody can prepare for

Whether the prophecy was ever real is decided separately — a hidden coin at game start under the default rule, or fixed by *Was There Ever a Prince?* — and revealed once, at the end. You can do everything right, be named by every priest in Westeros, and find out you were never him. Someone else might be, including a man nobody was watching.

## Practical advice

- Pick your character for the birth-marks. Five of nine are fixed at birth; a coastal-born Valyrian bastard of a duke starts halfway there.
- Equip the sword, do not collect it. Six Valyrian blades in a vault is zero marks. One in your hand is one.
- Marks are checked live, not stored. You can be named, lose your dragon, and be quietly un-named.
- Dying is a strategy. Make sure there is a capable red priest in your court first, and remember it costs him permanently.
